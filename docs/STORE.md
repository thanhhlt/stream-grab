## Package

Upload `releases/StreamGrab-v<version>.zip` (the files of `dist/` at the ZIP root, as for GitHub
releases). Every upload needs a higher `version` than the last one. The code is minified, not

## Store listing tab

**Name:** StreamGrab

**Summary** (the manifest `description`, 132 characters at most):

> Detect, download and merge online media streams entirely in your browser.

**Category:** Tools. **Language:** English.

**Description:**

```text
StreamGrab saves the videos a web page plays: it lists them as the page loads them, downloads
them in the background and joins video, audio and subtitles into one file, all inside your
browser. No server, no account, no extra program to install.

What it does
• Detects HLS (.m3u8) and MPEG-DASH (.mpd) streams, and MP4 and WebM files played by a page.
• Lets you pick the quality, the audio track and the subtitles, for one video or several at once.
• Downloads in the background: segments in parallel, automatic retries, resume after a browser
  restart, progress, speed and time left in the Queue tab.
• Joins video and audio into one MP4 (or MKV when MP4 cannot hold the codecs) with FFmpeg,
  bundled in the extension and run on your computer.
• Saves subtitles as .srt files next to the video, inside the video, or both.
• Saves to your Downloads folder, or a subfolder you choose.

What it does not do
• DRM-protected HLS and DASH streams (Widevine, PlayReady, FairPlay) are recognised and
  refused, never downloaded.
• YouTube is not supported.
• Live streams are not listed.

Use StreamGrab only for videos you are allowed to download, such as your own videos, videos
under a free license, or videos the site lets you save. Respect the site's terms and copyright.

Privacy
StreamGrab collects no data. It only connects to the sites a page itself loads video from, to
list and download those videos. Everything it stores stays in your browser.
Privacy policy: https://github.com/thanhhlt/stream-grab/blob/main/docs/PRIVACY.md

Free and open source
StreamGrab is free, with every feature, under the GNU GPL v3 or later. The bundled FFmpeg core
is GPL-2.0 or later.
Source code: https://github.com/thanhhlt/stream-grab
If it saves you time, you can support it on Ko-fi: https://ko-fi.com/s1lent91505
Nothing in the extension depends on it.
```

The donation line stays voluntary: no feature behind it and no wording that suggests one.

## Privacy practices tab

**Single purpose:**

> StreamGrab saves the videos a web page plays to the user's computer: it detects the page's
> video streams and files, downloads the ones the user picks, and joins them into one video file.

### Permission justifications

| Permission | Justification |
|---|---|
| `webRequest` | Watches the URL, status, Content-Type and Content-Length of the requests a tab makes, to recognise the video manifests (HLS, DASH), video files and subtitle files the page loads, and keeps the Referer and Origin of those media requests. Read only: requests are never blocked or changed through this API, and no other header is kept. |
| `declarativeNetRequestWithHostAccess` | Adds the page's Referer and Origin to the extension's own requests for a video's manifest, segments, video files and subtitles (session rules limited to requests made by the extension), because many video servers refuse requests without them. Requests of web pages are never changed. |
| `storage` | Keeps the user's settings, the videos detected in each tab and the download tasks. |
| `unlimitedStorage` | Downloaded segments of a video are kept in the extension's IndexedDB until they are merged, and the merged file (or a partly downloaded file) in the extension's private file system until Chrome saves it; a long video can be several gigabytes, more than the default quota allows. |
| `offscreen` | Runs the download engine and FFmpeg (WebAssembly, in a worker) in an offscreen document, because a service worker cannot host a Web Worker or hold large media in memory. |
| `downloads` | Saves the finished video and its subtitle files to the Downloads folder, shows them in the folder, and saves plain MP4/WebM files directly. |
| `notifications` | Tells the user when a video is saved or a download fails (the popup is usually closed by then); clicking it shows the file. |
| Host permissions `http://*/*`, `https://*/*` | Videos are played by any site. The extension must see a page's media requests to detect them, and fetch the manifest, segments and subtitles from the site that hosts them, wherever that is. |
| Content script on all http(s) pages | Reads the page title and title metadata (social and structured-data tags), the `<video>`, `<source>` and `<track>` elements with their labels, and the subtitle cues a player holds, so detected videos get a name, a duration and the right subtitles. It never runs page code. Not injected on YouTube sites. |

**Remote code:** No, I am not using remote code. All JavaScript and the FFmpeg WebAssembly core
are bundled in the package (`'wasm-unsafe-eval'` in the CSP only lets the bundled WebAssembly
run).

### Data usage

Collected data: tick **none** of the categories (personally identifiable information, health,
financial, authentication, personal communications, location, web history, user activity,
website content). The extension reads page requests and content only on the user's computer to
offer downloads; nothing is collected or transmitted to the developer.

## Notes for the reviewer

If the review asks how to test it:

> Open any page that plays an HLS or DASH video (for example https://hlsjs.video-dev.org/demo/),
> let it play for a few seconds, then click the StreamGrab icon: the video is listed in the
> Detected tab. Click Download; progress shows in the Queue tab and the file is saved to the
> Downloads folder. YouTube pages are not supported by design.
